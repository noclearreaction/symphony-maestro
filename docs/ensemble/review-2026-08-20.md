# Ensemble design review — 2026-08-20

> **Status:** Review findings, not decisions. Produced by an independent reviewer
> given [`README.md`](README.md), [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md) and
> [`../../spikes/acp/README.md`](../../spikes/acp/README.md), and briefed on which
> concepts had been removed or inverted during the design's evolution so that
> residue would count as a finding.
>
> Brief was: where does it contradict itself, is Ensemble thin, and what is
> asserted without support. Instructed to be blunt and not to praise.
>
> Annotations marked **[verified]** or **[disputed]** are mine, added after
> checking against the repository.

---

## 1. Internal contradictions

### A. The deleted per-persona cap survives in three places

`§Vocabulary` defines a persona as including "how many may sound at once".
`§Why a part, and not a person` lists "Per-persona concurrency cap" as what
provides bounded team size. `§Tool surface` has `personas()` returning "caps".
`§What bounds concurrency` says "There is deliberately **no per-persona cap**".

The class diagram sides with §3 — `Persona` carries `credential`, no concurrency
field — so the diagram and the vocabulary table disagree. Worst instance because
the residue is in the definition of the core term and in the tool that reports it:
anyone building `personas()` from the surface table ships the deleted concept.

**[verified]** All three sites present.

### B. Scenario A cannot happen — the auditor has nothing to read

Scenario A step 8 has the auditor "get what it needs from the worktrees and the
specs". `§Channels` says a part worktree is "Not readable by another part" and
"An auditor reading a developer's work reads the change branch, not the
developer's tree". At step 8 nothing has been accepted, so the change branch is
empty.

The channel rule — a part's work becomes visible to others only by merging —
makes review-before-merge impossible, and review-before-merge is the point of the
scenario.

**[verified]** This is the most serious finding: not a story simplification but
the model failing its own worked example.

### C. The principal is declared a part, and the model cannot hold one

`§2`: "The principal is not privileged infrastructure. It is the part you land
in." Against that:

- `§1`: "A part never holds one [an approval gate]" — the principal holds it
- `§Who owns git`: "a part never touches git" — the principal owns change-level git
- `§Lifecycle`: the principal never reaches `Queued`, `Playing`, `Merging` or
  `Merged`, so under "merged or reverted are the only two ends" it has no legal
  end state, and arguably no legal state
- The architecture diagram routes `A1 --> HOOK` but has no `P1 --> HOOK`, drawing
  the widest-ceiling part outside enforcement

Either the principal is a distinct kind of thing, or every "a part never X"
statement is false.

**[verified]** Residue of collapsing the old `player` concept.

### D. Parts without a worktree have no terminal state

`Persona` carries `worktree: bool`; the architecture diagram shows an auditor
part without one. A worktree-less part can never merge a branch, yet Scenario A
step 9 asserts `rate-limit-review` reaches `Merged` — and its output goes to the
outbox "referenced, not committed", so its branch is empty by construction.

`§Vocabulary` also defines a part as "a transcript, a worktree, a lifecycle, a
completion", false for exactly these parts.

**[verified]** The completion model covers only code-producing work.

### E. `Attached` has no exit to queued work

`§Lifecycle` has only `Attached --> AwaitingReview: detach()`. But `§3` says
queued work "starts when the human detaches", and Scenario D step 5 says "You
detach. The queued work starts." That needs `Attached --> Queued`, which does not
exist. Scenario D step 4 also calls `resume` on an `Attached` part, an undefined
transition.

### F. The mutex rules contradict the worktree rules and each other

1. "Mutexes are acquired atomically at dispatch" versus "A part's worktree is
   created at enqueue, not at selection". If the worktree *is* the mutex, it is
   taken at enqueue.
2. "Avoid by sharing a worktree" versus `§Channels` "No space is writable by two
   parts". The cheapest conflict-avoidance tier is prohibited by the channel
   invariant.
3. "Held for the life of the part" means a part idling in `AwaitingReview` holds
   its worktree indefinitely, starving anything waiting on it. The offered proof
   ("no hold-and-wait, therefore no deadlock") addresses simultaneous acquisition,
   not indefinite holding.
4. Mutexes appear in no tool signature, no class and no state. Nothing can
   declare one.

Points 2 and 3 also reintroduce the deleted **precondition** mechanism under
another name — a queued part blocked on another part's completion is a dependency
edge — while `§Starting is not playing` insists "There is no dependency graph".

### G. Conflict re-apply does what the mutex rule exists to prevent

`§Merged or reverted`: "Ensemble resets the part's worktree to the moved base".
`§What bounds concurrency` justifies part-lifetime mutexes because "a worktree
that shifts between turns leaves a part re-entering a checkout its own transcript
no longer matches". The re-apply mechanism shifts it deliberately. It also makes
Ensemble a writer of a space whose declared writer is the part.

### H. The grant expires before dispatch

`§6` diagram says "TTL 15m"; `§Invariants` says the grant is "minted when work is
queued". With work waiting behind chairs — Scenario C has three parts waiting —
the grant expires before the part is ever played.

Separately, "a part that can re-sign its grant has disarmed the policy" is
incoherent: with Ed25519 the part holds no private key. The other threats listed
(write `.vibe/hooks.toml`, replace the hook binary) are real; this one misdirects
the trust-boundary analysis.

**[verified]** Both.

### I. `cancel` breaks the two-ends model; `Reverted` means four things

`cancel` is described as keeping "the transcript for inspection" while the
lifecycle sends it to `Reverted`, where "nothing is left behind". `Reverted` now
covers conflict-unresolvable, cancelled-before-play, cancelled-mid-turn and
given-up-after-`Faulted` — four outcomes with different consequences behind one
word the document calls clean.

### J. Ground rule 2 is derived from a rule about a different kind of process

`§1` reasons: "Only user-facing agents can ask questions. Subagents return text
and cannot prompt → A part never holds one." But a part is a top-level ACP
session, not a subagent.

**[disputed — conclusion right, derivation wrong]** `vibe/acp/agent.py` disables
`ask_user_question` via `NON_INTERACTIVE_DISABLED_TOOLS`, so a part genuinely
cannot ask. The reviewer's claim that the substrate permits asking is wrong. But
it is right that the stated reasoning does not support the conclusion, and that
the correct evidence is elsewhere. Permission requests *do* reach the client via
`client.request_permission()`, which is a different thing and unexercised in the
spike.

### K. The stated cost of replacing the base prompt is wrong

`§Instruction snippets` claims that replacing the base means reintroducing "the
scratchpad rules, OS and shell, which subagents exist". Those are fragments 5, 7
and 8, gated by `include_prompt_detail` and appended *after* the base.
`system_prompt_id` replaces fragment 1 only.

**[verified]** Checked all three fragment docs. This was the stated justification
for Ensemble owning a snippet library, and it is false. The contract half of that
justification still stands.

### L. Three names for one knob, meaningless for one agent

`Persona.profile`, `VIBE_DEFAULT_AGENT` and `system_prompt_id` are the same lever
under three names. And Claude Code takes `_meta.systemPrompt`, so
`Persona.profile` means nothing for a Claude-backed persona — a schema presented
as protocol-neutral is Vibe-shaped, and Scenario A bakes `VIBE_DEFAULT_AGENT`
into the generic flow.

### M. Smaller contradictions

- **"Through `wait`, and only through `wait`"** is falsified on the same page by
  `result`, `status`, `parts` and `start_sync`. The intended claim is "there is no
  push".
- **`start_sync` cannot honour the no-hang invariant** — returns `Result[]` with
  no `pending` shape.
- **Batch `accept` has no order**, yet "ordering falls out of completion order".
  Scenario A accepts three at once and asserts all merge clean, with no account of
  how three trees merged into one branch without touching each other.
- **Architecture diagram** shows `attach` only to the principal and `plays` only
  to two of three parts.
- **`Work.budget` and `Work.priority` are unsettable** by any tool signature, yet
  `Faulted: budget exceeded` depends on a budget.
- **Scenario B step 1 uses the deleted definition of completion** — "played to
  completion… the transcript is final", then resumed in step 3. In the scenario
  written specifically to police the model.
- **Scenario A branch names** are `change/rate-limit-impl`, against
  `§Two levels of git`'s `part/<name>` under `change/<name>`. Scenario A has no
  change branch at all.
- **Base drift was deleted but is load-bearing twice** — the enqueue-time
  worktree justification depends on the base moving, and enqueue-time branching
  maximises drift (branch earliest, merge latest) without the trade being argued.
- **"Nothing left behind"** is contradicted by `§6` preferring worktrees left
  intact for inspection, by `§Faulted`, and by the concession that a part with
  `bash` can write anywhere.
- **`.vibe/hooks.toml` is worse than stated** — a duplicate hook name means "the
  project entry wins", so a part writing a project-level hook *displaces* the
  policy hook rather than adding to it; and hooks load only for a trusted folder,
  which a fresh part worktree may not be.
- **Constitution §5 conflict** — `§Who owns git` argues for raw git over a tool
  wrapping `gh pr create`, while `governance/constitution.md` §5 requires the most
  specific purpose-built tool and permits shell "only when no structured tool
  exists". The document cites the constitution for the `main` gate while
  contradicting it here without acknowledgement.

## 2. Is Ensemble thin? — No

The document says "Ensemble owns four things and nothing else". By the end it
also owns the queue, chairs, section reservation, per-credential rate accounting,
mutexes, worktree creation, commits, commit-message validation, merges, conflict
artefact construction, worktree resets, reverts, a snippet composition library, an
inbox, an outbox, an event-stream API, per-work budgets, cost accounting and a
three-frontend plan.

Ranked by weight shed:

**1. The grant token and the policy engine.** The document's own observation kills
it: "Ensemble *is* the issuer". The hook is a process Ensemble spawned, on the
same machine, and Ensemble already serves HTTP for MCP — so the hook can simply
ask "may part P run this call?". That removes `Grant`, `ENSEMBLE_GRANT`, Ed25519,
the TTL, the re-signing threat, and the whole of §7 including a six-row engine
comparison for a decision the section then defers.

**2. Ensemble-owned merge, conflict re-apply and revert.** Change-level git is
already the principal's, and it "is already sitting in the parent worktree" with
git and judgment. Merging a part branch is one command; a conflict is a `resume`
message it writes. The load-bearing premise is self-flagged as unverified, and
its named fallback needs none of the machinery.

**3. Terminal brokering, `attach`, and the three-frontend plan.** `§1` already
hands this to the substrate: ACP sessions are ordinary Vibe sessions, so a human
can attach with `vibe --resume <id>`. Attach is then printing a session id. The
residual requirement — don't let the scheduler drive a session a human is in — is
one boolean. Deleting the broker also dissolves the principal paradox.

**4. The scheduler beyond a semaphore.** A queue of ready work plus a concurrency
limit *is* a semaphore. `priority` is consumed by nothing, mutexes are incoherent,
and surplus-sharing is an optimisation with no stated demand. Per-credential rate
limiting is provider backpressure, and rubato is already on the API path.

**5. Sections.** **[disputed]** The reviewer proposes deleting the concept. The
user has explicitly asked for long-lived sections with their own principals, so
this is out of scope — though `max_changes_in_flight` and the
sections-reaching-each-other question are fair targets.

**6. The instruction-snippet library.** Ensemble shipping composable prompt
fragments with composition rules is a prompt framework on top of an agent that
already has one. The stated justification is false (§1K). What is owed is one
block of contract text per agent kind, in configuration.

**7. The outbox.** A second artefact store with its own lifecycle, plus a snippet
teaching every part to route output between two stores by judgment. A
`.gitignore`d path in the worktree gets the same property with no new space and no
judgment call.

**8. `start_sync`, and the `wait`/`status`/`result` overlap.** Four read tools
where two would do — which matters because §4's premise is that every tool
boundary costs a turn.

**9. Budget and cost.** Settable by no tool, contradicts "none reachable from a
tool", duplicates accounting belonging to rubato or the provider.

**10. The event-stream subscription.** ACP already emits `session/update`, and the
session log is a file on disk. Observation is `tail -f`.

## 3. Asserted without support

1. **`§1`'s blanket "verified against the shipped binary" does not cover the row
   the design rests on.** Session resumability was verified for the *CLI*; the ACP
   form is `session/load`, which the substrate section concedes is unverified and
   the spike lists as not covered. The consequence drawn from it — "A part *is* a
   session. Every follow-up is a resume" — is the foundation of parts, resume,
   review and conflict re-apply.
2. **"Termination is structural rather than hoped for"** — the work set is not
   finite. The document itself says more work gets enqueued, and Scenario A adds a
   part mid-flight. Termination depends on the orchestrator choosing to stop.
3. **"No hold-and-wait and therefore no deadlock"** — correct and irrelevant to
   the hazard the same paragraph creates.
4. **"Adequate while parts are fallible rather than adversarial"** — the
   load-bearing security decision, supported by one sentence, ignoring that a
   fallible part reading untrusted content behaves adversarially.
5. **"The provider actually limits this — per account, not per persona"** — sole
   justification for deleting the per-persona cap, with no provider named and no
   number.
6. **Ed25519 and TTL 15m** appear only as diagram labels, with no reasoning
   recorded and the TTL falsified by the queue.
7. **"Cached input bills at a tenth of fresh"**, **"MCP requests time out at both
   ends"**, **"60s working default"** — no sources. And Scenario C has the
   principal polling `wait(until: all)` across dozens of 60-second turns, each
   re-sending the whole context, which the document's own turn-cost argument makes
   the dominant expense and never mentions.
8. **"A chair spends nearly all its time waiting on a model API"** — unmeasured,
   and contradicted by a `tester` persona running a suite.
9. **The Anthropic terms quote** — no link, document name or date, supporting a
   durable conclusion and a build-order decision.
10. **The spike's own inference is backwards.** It concludes "the profile is doing
    more than swapping the base prompt — 8,617 chars". The bundled base is 9,234
    chars and `reviewer.md` is 538: an 8,696 delta accounting for essentially all
    of it. **[verified]** The measurement is fine, the inference is wrong, and §1
    imports the spike as measured ground.
11. **Negative claims about documentation** — "the published docs are silent",
    "Mistral documents none of it" — unverifiable as written and load-bearing for
    the risk framing.
12. **Decisions recorded without their reasoning** — the Cedar/Biscuit deferral
    when Biscuit's differentiator has already been eliminated; Biscuit kept live
    with its npm gap "wanting explaining"; the restart choice identified as
    needing a decision and not decided; VS Code hedges inside a table that reads
    as a plan; and the scenarios disclaimed as illustrative while being the only
    specification of batch `accept`, part branch naming, cross-part visibility and
    detach-releases-queued-work.
